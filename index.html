What do the phases mean?

Phase 1 · Safety & dose
Usually the first studies in people. Researchers check side effects, dose levels and how the body handles the treatment.

Phase 2 · Early effectiveness
Studies in people with the condition explore whether the treatment may help, while continuing to assess safety.

Phase 3 · Larger confirmation
Larger studies usually compare benefits and risks with a placebo or another treatment. Results may support an approval application; success does not guarantee approval.

Phase 4 · After approval
Research after a treatment is approved can examine longer-term safety and how it works in routine use. Not every post-approval study is Phase 4.

Combined phases & 1a/1b
Phase 1/2 combines stages in one study. Labels such as 1a and 1b describe parts of a Phase 1 program; their exact goals depend on the trial.

Extensions & uncertain labels
An extension follows participants longer; it is not automatically a new pivotal trial or Phase 4. “Phase not stated” means the source did not verify the phase.

A phase describes the study’s purpose, not its chance of success. Study size and design vary. FDA: clinical research phases
